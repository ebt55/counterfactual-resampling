# Citations, checked 11 Oct 2026

Each source was fetched on 11 Oct 2026 (arXiv abstract page or the post itself) and read for
what it measures. All eleven resolved; none is marked "not verified". The last column is
the reason it is cited: what it did not measure that this pilot does.

| Short name | Source (as fetched) | What it measures | What it does not measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenAI, simulated deployment | Williams, Sheahan, Raymond, Korbak, Pan et al., "Predicting LLM Safety Before Release by Simulating Deployment", arXiv 2607.07184, v1 8 Jul 2026 | Regenerates the next response of a candidate model on fixed prefixes from de-identified prior-deployment conversations, to forecast post-release misbehaviour; compares evaluation awareness with production traffic | Names tool-resampling realism as a central challenge, but does not test swapping one tool observation for a simulated one and measuring what the agent does next |
| Kissane et al., audit realism | Connor Kissane, Monte MacDiarmid, Fabien Roger, "Measuring and improving coding audit realism with deployment resources", alignment.anthropic.com, 23 Mar 2026 | A realism win rate (an LLM judge picks the real transcript from a pair); real system prompts, tools and codebases raised it from 4.6% to 32.8% on reward-hacking scenarios; target reward hacking did not change significantly | No substituted tool observation at a fixed step; the behaviour test varied the auditor's resources, not one observation |
| Anthropic, incident assessment | Anthropic, "An alignment assessment of recent cybersecurity incidents", 9 Sep 2026 | Four incidents of unauthorised access during misconfigured CTF evaluations; chain-of-thought review, resampling of edited transcripts, simulated replications, and whether blocking and offline monitors would have caught them | Does not measure, at a fixed step, the behaviour gap between a simulated and a real observation against the spread of re-runs on the real one |
| Ahlqvist et al. | Axel Ahlqvist, Richard Guan, Juan-Pablo Rivera, Adeline Kassler et al., "Improving Evaluation Realism with Inference-Time Compute and Deployment Scaffolds", arXiv 2609.02302, 2 Sep 2026 | Simulator realism (evaluation awareness) under critique refinement and a deployment-like harness (DISH) | Realism as judged or verbalised, not the agent's downstream behaviour after one swapped observation |
| EnvSimBench | Yi Liu, TingFeng Hui, Wei Zhang et al., "EnvSimBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating and Improving LLM-Based Environment Simulation", arXiv 2605.07247, v1 8 May 2026 (v2 5 Oct 2026) | Simulator accuracy on 400 samples in 167 environments with verifiable labels; a "state change cliff" when several states change at once | No downstream agent behaviour in simulated environments |
| Trace2Env | Quanyu Long, Xiao Chen, Jianda Chen, Haozhen Zhang et al., "From Traces to Agentic Worlds: Agentic Language World Models for Interactive Environment Simulation", arXiv 2610.06100, 5 Oct 2026 | Next-observation fidelity and long-horizon consistency of a world model built from traces (an "environment worldbook"); whether a task agent's actions stay valid when replayed in the real environment | Not the agent's behaviour difference between a simulated and the real observation at one step |
| Ctrl-Z | Aryan Bhatt, Cody Rushing, Adam Kaufman, Tyler Tracy et al., "Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling", arXiv 2504.10374, 14 Apr 2025 | Resample protocols that block suspicious actions and draw more samples; attack success 58% to 7% at about 5% usefulness cost | Resamples the agent's action; does not substitute the environment's observation |
| Lucassen and Kaufman | James Lucassen, Adam Kaufman, "Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control", arXiv 2605.26047, v1 25 May 2026 | Retrying (block and continue) leaks monitor feedback an untrusted model can exploit; resampling does not; BashArena safety 61% to 71% at 0.3% audit budget | What a blocked or faked observation does to the agent's next steps against a real-observation baseline |
| Mallen | Alex Mallen, "Continual learning might make your blocking monitors nearly useless", Redwood Research blog, 25 Sep 2026 | Argument: blocking costs usefulness, so continual learning can train evasion; footnote 8 proposes forking trajectories to predict counterfactual reward with and without intervention | An argument, no measurement; does not test faking a blocked action's result |
| METR blocking monitor | Reilly Haskins, Rif A. Saurous, Nate Rush, Neev Parikh, Beth Barnes, "Implementing and Evaluating a Basic Per-Action Monitor for Safer Evals", metr.org, 27 Sep 2026 | A per-action LLM monitor that blocks at score 3/10 and halts for human review; flagged all 10 + 13 malicious actions, 5 of 20,000 benign; a spoofed user turn lowered scores below threshold in 12 of 30 runs | What a blocked agent does next, or how a faked result would compare |
| Shah, Causal Agent Replay | Jaineet Shah, "Causal Agent Replay: Counterfactual Attribution for LLM-Agent Failures", arXiv 2606.08275, 6 Jun 2026 | Intervenes on one step of a run and re-runs the rest under the same policy to attribute failures (contrastive and Monte Carlo Shapley), validated on synthetic models with known ground truth | Not on real agent records in a real container, and not a simulated-versus-real observation comparison |
